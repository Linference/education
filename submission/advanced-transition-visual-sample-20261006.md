# GEZHI advanced transitions: visual sample

12 seconds, 60 fps, three transitions of 4 seconds each. The sample compares only the relationship between camera and graphics; it does not yet include narration or music; it uses existing character assets and a mock interface. The feedback text in the film is used to explain the motion relationships and does not represent actual AI responses or student trial results.

## 01 Spatial Push: spatial push-in

Five roles are located in the same perspective space. The selected card expands into a learning room, the other cards recede along the depth axis, and the figures' positions and scale change continuously. The sense of space comes from foreground/background occlusion, perspective, and focus changes.

Used for the roughly 10–20 second role-selection section of the formal film; it comes to a stop and then cuts to actual software recording.

## 02 Evidence Match: original-words match

One quoted sentence from the explanation stays in the camera's focus, the surrounding input interface changes into a feedback report, the same sentence becomes the quoted evidence, and then the next-step suggestion appears.

This is the main transition most worth keeping: the motion directly explains the "explanation → evidence → improvement" innovation rather than merely decorating it. Used for roughly 20–35 seconds.

## 03 Lightfield Fold: six-dimensional convergence

The six-dimensional outline leaves the plane, its vertices and light trails rotate and converge in depth, the GEZHI wordmark appears from blur to solid, and finally a stable image remains.

Used for the roughly 55–60 second ending. The final graphic can be adjusted once the Logo is finalized.

## Texture control for the formal film

The main transitions use a consistent camera direction, inertia, and lighting; fast motion is kept at the points where the image hands off, and the motion decelerates and settles when core information is displayed. Deep graphite, soft white, pale purple, and teal run throughout. UI type and screenshots stay sharp, and figures keep their original proportions.

The score keeps the aforementioned 96 BPM cinematic electronic direction; spatial push-in is paired with low-frequency propulsion and light airy sound, original-words match keeps a short pause, and the ending converges with overtones and harmony.
