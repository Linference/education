## 3. Language Red Line (The Most Error-Prone Item)

[Fact] Charter original text: **All registration materials, work content, attachments, posters, video subtitles, demonstration presentations and defenses are accepted in English only.**
Proper names such as school names and student names may be filled in using their official English translations.

**The places to check are more detailed than you might imagine**:

- [ ] **Every single word** on the poster (including annotations inside images, chart axis labels, and the description next to the QR code)
- [ ] The video's **narration and subtitles** (**subtitles count too**)
- [ ] The **software interface** shown in the video — if Gezhi's UI is in Chinese, it will be directly exposed in the video
- [ ] PPT / presentation slides (needed for the second review and the final)
- [ ] Chart titles, legends and code comments in the report
- [ ] The file names themselves

> 💡 **Practical advice**: if the video is to show an app with a Chinese interface, **narrate in English** and put **English annotations** on screen, and also prepare a set of **English interface screenshots** for the report. Don't make the judges guess at a screen full of Chinese.
> In addition, the Chinese brand name "Gezhi" (written in Chinese characters) may be retained on the poster as a **visual element** (it is a proper name), but **the English mark `Gezhi` must be provided at the same time** — this is also why it was previously suggested that formal submissions use the horizontal lockup of "icon + GEZHI".

## 4. Working Backwards from the Scoring Rubric: What Each Material Should Emphasize

| Judging area | Weight | Main material for earning points | What this corresponds to on Gezhi's side |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Problem and educational value | 20% | Introduction + report | [Fact] Bastani 2024 (AI giving answers: exam scores −17%), Khanmigo RCT (students sought help in only 17% of incorrect-answer sessions) → see `research\07` |
| Creativity and originality | 20% | Introduction + report | The "fallible junior schoolmate" + clearly state the differences from existing solutions (**do not say "world's first"**) |
| **Application of AI technology** | **25%** | Report | DSH kernel, local knowledge-base retrieval, Feynman scoring rubric, **students must be able to explain it on the spot** |
| Prototype, testing and effectiveness | 20% | Report + video | **There must be real trial evidence**: find 3–5 classmates to try it, record changes in Feynman scores / how many rounds it took to convince the junior schoolmate |
| English expression and presentation | 10% | All materials + defense | Complete structure, accurate terminology |
| Ethics, safety and privacy | 5% | Report | Local-first data, consent and anonymization for real-person testing, **list of AI tools** |

> 🎯 **The two highest-weighted items (AI technology application 25% + prototype testing 20% = 45%) both depend on "evidence", not "description".**
> The charter's original requirements for these two are "**students can explain clearly**" and "**evidence supporting the effectiveness and directions for improvement**".
> Therefore: **trial records + AI usage record table are the two things most worth adding in these 8 days.**

- [ ] All 4 files present: introduction / report / poster / video (or video link)
- [ ] File names match the rules in section 2 **character by character**
- [ ] Each PDF **≤ 50 MB** (the poster easily exceeds this; do not blindly push image resolution to 600 dpi)

**Content**
- [ ] All materials contain **no Chinese characters** (except proper names)

- [ ] All data, images, code and articles have sources; open-source content states its license

- [ ] No **API keys, plaintext secrets or real student names** appear anywhere

**Final step**
- [ ] **On a different computer / in an incognito window**, download each file back from the registration system and open it — confirm that the judges can open it, that the layout is intact and that no fonts are missing
