## 2. Competition Overview

- Initiated by the UNU Global AI Network, the "AI for Sustainable Development Goals — Global Youth AI Future Innovation Competition" aims to encourage young people to use artificial intelligence to respond to sustainable development challenges and to promote social innovation and global youth development.
- The Macau Secondary School Region is aimed at local secondary school students. It is based on the principle of matching the research, design and programming capabilities of the secondary school stage, and encourages students to start from real educational needs and complete **AI works that are operable, demonstrable and capable of basic testing**.
- The 2026 theme is "AI and Education: AI Transforming the Educational Paradigm and Enhancing AI Literacy", developed along two directions:
  - using AI to improve education;
  - enhancing the AI literacy of learners, teachers and communities.
- The charter emphasizes that works should centre on educational contexts and explain their relationship to the United Nations Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs).

## 4. Competition Themes and Project Directions (Choose One of Three)

1. **AI for Education**: Use artificial intelligence to improve teaching, learning and educational management, making education more personalized, convenient, inclusive and effective. Examples for reference: intelligent tutoring, adaptive learning, curriculum and textbook generation, automated assessment and feedback, learning early warning, language learning, accessible learning, teacher professional development, or school resource optimization.
2. **AI for Social Innovation**: Use artificial intelligence to address social issues such as educational equity, digital inclusion, youth wellbeing, community learning or public services. Works should define the target users, actual needs and expected social impact.
3. **AI for Less Developed Countries**: Design low-cost, easily accessible AI education solutions that fit the local context for resource-limited, remote or underserved regions, such as offline learning tools, mobile-first technology, teacher training, local-language learning, voice or SMS services, and lightweight infrastructure solutions.

## 5. Eligibility and Team Composition

- Entry is free of charge.
- Participants must be students enrolled in a Macao secondary school at registration and during the competition.
- Schools register as teams; **each school may enter at most five teams**, and each team may consist of at most **3 students from the same school**, led by **1 to 2 supervising teachers**. The organizers may adjust quotas depending on registration.
- Each student may join only one team; each team may submit only one work.
- Works may be completed under teacher guidance, but the conception, research, system design, data processing and demonstration should be student-led; judges may verify the students' actual participation through the defense.

---

## 6. Level of Work and Technical Requirements

- The basic requirement is an "operable prototype" that secondary school students can complete; **there is no requirement to establish a company, achieve commercialization or use large professional equipment**. Works may be a website, mobile application, chatbot, data analysis tool, computer vision system, smart hardware or other AI solution.
- Works must include **actually working AI functionality**; it is not acceptable to submit only a concept, a slide deck, an ordinary database system, or a simulation of AI using fixed rules alone.
- Teams must demonstrate an operable prototype and complete basic testing in at least a simulated or small-scale real educational setting, for example trial use by classmates, teachers or target users.
- Teams must explain the models, platforms, data sources, prompt design or training methods used, and the specific role of AI in the overall system.
- If a work is recommended for linkage to the global competition, the team must supplement materials such as technical maturity, real-world validation, and market or scaling potential in accordance with the latest global competition rules. Where the global competition rules are inconsistent with this charter, the official global competition rules prevail.

## 7. Submission Materials and Format

**Important language requirement**: For the 2026 edition, all registration materials, work content, attachments, posters, video subtitles, demonstration presentations and defenses are accepted in English only. Proper names such as school names and student names may be filled in using their official English translations.

- **Project Introduction**: one English project introduction, recommended not to exceed 2 pages, explaining the problem, target users, main features, AI methods, innovative points and expected impact.
- **Research Report**: one English research report, recommended 6 to 12 pages, covering background, problem definition, solution design, technical methods, data sources, testing process, results, limitations, ethical and privacy considerations, and references.
- **Poster**: one English poster, portrait orientation, 0.8 m wide by 1.1 m high, with both text and images, capable of explaining the key points of the work on its own.
- **Demonstration Materials**: photographs of the work and an English demonstration video of no more than 5 minutes; the video must have clear English narration or English explanation.
- All text documents and posters are submitted as PDF, with each file no larger than 50 MB; videos are uploaded in the format specified by the registration system, or a publicly accessible link is provided.

**File naming rules**

```
ProjectIntroduction_ProjectName.pdf
ResearchReport_ProjectName.pdf
Poster_ProjectName.pdf
DemoVideo_ProjectName.mp4 (or a video link file)
```

## 9. Judging Criteria

| Judging area | Weight | Focus |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Problem and educational value | 20% | The problem is clear and addresses real educational needs, and the target users and SDG linkage can be explained. |
| Creativity and originality | 20% | The conception is creative, and the differences from existing solutions can be explained. |
| Application of AI technology | 25% | The AI functionality plays a substantive role; the methods are appropriate and students can explain them clearly. |
| Prototype, testing and effectiveness | 20% | The prototype is operable; the testing method is reasonable, with evidence supporting the effectiveness and directions for improvement. |
| English expression and presentation | 10% | The English materials are clear and well structured, and the demonstration and defense accurately convey the work. |
| Ethics, safety and privacy | 5% | Data, bias, copyright, safety and responsible use of AI are handled properly. |

## 10. Final Presentation and Defense

- Shortlisted teams must bring an operable work or a version suitable for on-site demonstration, and use an English poster and English presentation for the demonstration.
- The demonstration and defense time, equipment specifications and venue restrictions for each team will be notified separately by the organizers.
- **All three students should participate in the demonstration or defense**. Judges may require on-the-spot operation, explanation of the program or model, explanation of data sources, and answers to questions about the originality of the work.
- If the work involves network services, teams must prepare an offline video, screenshots or a backup demonstration plan, so that network or third-party platform failures do not affect the demonstration.

## 12. Use of Artificial Intelligence, Originality and Academic Integrity

- Participants may use generative AI, open-source models, APIs and third-party platforms to assist research or development, but must list in the research report the tool names, versions (where applicable), purposes, and the parts completed by students.
- Works must be led and completed by the participating students. It is forbidden to pass off others' works, purchase finished products, have someone else do the work, or submit a system that students cannot reasonably explain and operate.
- All data, images, code, models, articles and audiovisual materials must be legally obtained and properly cited. When using open-source content, its license terms must be observed.
- It is forbidden to upload personal data without permission, sensitive student data, or personally identifiable images. When real-person testing is involved, schools must obtain appropriate consent and take anonymization and safety measures.
- In cases of plagiarism, false data, concealment of AI use or other serious violations, the organizers may cancel eligibility for participation or awards.

## 13. Intellectual Property, Licensing and Personal Data

- The original intellectual property of the work belongs to the participants or their lawful rights holders. Participants must guarantee that the work does not infringe the intellectual property, privacy or other lawful rights and interests of third parties.
- For the purposes of competition judging, display, promotion, education and event records, participants agree that the organizers may use the work title, abstract, poster, photographs and demonstration clips on a non-exclusive and royalty-free basis; if undisclosed technical materials are involved, teams should clearly mark them at the time of submission.
- Schools must ensure that consent from students and their parents or guardians for registration, filming, public display and personal data processing has been obtained in accordance with applicable regulations.

## 15. One-Page Highlights (Action Checklist for Participating Teams)

1. **Confirm thematic fit**: the work must centre on an educational context and correspond to the SDGs.
2. **Form a team and register**: 1–3 students from the same school + 1–2 supervising teachers; each school may enter at most 5 teams; registration form https://form.mcs.mo/forms/global-youth-ai-competition-pqnoz0
3. **Build an operable prototype**: it must have genuinely working AI functionality, and at least one small-scale real/simulated test must be completed, with test evidence retained.
4. **Prepare four English materials**: introduction (≤2 pages), research report (6–12 pages), portrait poster (0.8×1.1 m), demonstration video (≤5 minutes, with English narration), submitted under the prescribed file names.
5. **Keep to the timeline**: submit by 9 October → preliminary review 10–30 October → second review 1–15 November (online English defense) → offline final in Macao on 26 November.
6. **Hold the integrity bottom line**: truthfully list in the report the names, versions (where applicable), purposes of generative AI tools and the parts completed by students themselves; cite materials lawfully; obtain consent and anonymize real-person testing.
7. **Prepare for the defense**: all three students must be able to operate the work on the spot and explain the model, data sources and originality; where network services are involved, prepare an offline plan.
